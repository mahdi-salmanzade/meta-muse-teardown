# Why this research exists

[Overview](README.md) · [Findings](CROSS-PLATFORM.md) · [Research history](RESEARCH-HISTORY.md)

This project grew out of the repo owner's concern about privacy and the power that comes with access to people's everyday lives. Julian Assange's warnings about internet surveillance helped shape that concern. A personal agent makes the questions concrete: what can it read, what does it remember, who controls that information, and whose data is included besides the account holder's?

> The internet is the 'greatest spying machine the world has ever seen' and is not a technology that necessarily favours the freedom of speech.
>
> — Julian Assange's remarks, as summarized by [The Guardian, 15 March 2011](https://www.theguardian.com/media/2011/mar/15/web-spying-machine-julian-assange). The full sentence is the article's summary, not a verbatim sentence from his speech.

The author's phrase **“spyware by design”** is an editorial privacy assessment of broad access, persistent sync and control over personal data. It is not a demonstrated malware classification, a finding of malicious intent, or proof of covert collection or an OS-permission bypass. The [dated assessment](SPYWARE-VERDICT-2026-09-27.md) and its [independent corrections](TODAYS-CHANGES-AUDIT-2026-09-27.md) remain available.

The research should stand on inspectable evidence, including evidence that narrows or refutes a concern. That means giving removed capabilities and stronger controls the same visibility as newly discovered risks, preserving corrections, and stating when native behavior has not been measured.

Earlier historical context about Meta is preserved in the [original report](reports/README-BEFORE-RESTRUCTURE.md#metas-track-record). Those events are context, not evidence that Muse repeats them. The repository's banner and other editorial artwork are not application screenshots or technical evidence.

Independent research by the repo owner; not affiliated with Meta. Corrections are welcome through repository issues with the affected build, claim and supporting material.
